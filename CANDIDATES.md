# Candidates — The Word You'd Delete From the Dictionary

The entry is ONE word only (gate: 1–77 UTF-8 characters, not only whitespace).
The judge compares words head to head: funniest deletion wins. Criteria, in order:
real recognisable word; funny at first sight; humour from the word itself (sound,
double meaning, the gap it leaves); fresh (not the field's first thought);
exact, one word, spelled correctly.

## 10 candidates

1. **moist** — the most hated word in English; deleting it pleases everyone instantly. (Rejected: the field will flood with it; zero freshness, byte-identical copies share one rate.)
2. **literally** — deleting it ends a century of misuse; every pedant cheers. (Rejected: another field favourite, joke is about usage, not the word itself.)
3. **irregardless** — the dictionary's most famous non-word, a double negative (ir- + regardless) that contradicts itself; deleting it finally fixes the dictionary. (CHOSEN)
4. **defenestration** — the word for throwing things out of a window; deleting it IS a defenestration of the word itself. (Runner-up: self-enacting joke, very fresh, but needs one extra inference step to land.)
5. **abbreviation** — a long word meaning "making things short"; deleting it is the ultimate abbreviation. (Strong: instant, ironic, fresh — but the joke is a touch gentler than #3.)
6. **monosyllabic** — a five-syllable word meaning "one syllable"; the word violates its own meaning. (Strong and fresh, but slightly narrower recognition than #3.)
7. **lisp** — a word people with a lisp cannot pronounce; deleting it is a kindness. (Rejected: a well-known observation, low freshness.)
8. **sesquipedalian** — a long word meaning "given to long words"; deleting it removes the word for itself. (Rejected: needs the reader to know the meaning first; joke lands slower.)
9. **aglet** — the plastic tip of a shoelace; almost nobody knows the word, so deleting it changes nothing. (Rejected: the joke IS that nothing happens — weak at first sight.)
10. **supercalifragilisticexpialidocious** — deleting the longest fun word in the language. (Rejected: the joke is only "it's long"; the field will think of it too.)

## Pick: `irregardless`

Why it wins head to head:
- **Instant:** every English reader knows it and knows the argument about it — the joke lands in under a second, no caption needed.
- **Intrinsic:** the humour comes from the word itself — a double negative that should not exist, yet sits in the dictionary. Deleting it is the dictionary correcting itself.
- **Fresh enough:** the field's first thoughts are "moist", "literally", "Monday", "taxes". "irregardless" is the pedants' pick, not the crowd's.
- **Exact:** one word, 12 characters, spelled correctly, no explanation attached.

Final entry text: `irregardless`
